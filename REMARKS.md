### Where the descriptions can teach the wrong lesson

- **Slides 8–10 — explain the application, not just the illustration.** For 8, use an example such as estimating a catalyst's electronic energy; a research target is not a demonstrated near-term advantage in drug discovery. For 9, give a concrete climate trade-off (routing for emissions under delivery deadlines); distinguish a hybrid quantum/classical method from a *quantum-inspired* method that runs entirely classically. For 10, the actionable point is that post-quantum cryptography runs on **classical** hardware now. Distinguish migration from the future fault-tolerant threat to RSA/ECC. [NIST's standards](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/post-quantum-cryptography) give concrete examples: ML-KEM for key establishment, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA for signatures.
- **Slides 11, 13 and 52–54 — a superposition is not two observed values, and matching bits do not prove entanglement.** The spin drawing uses an unweighted `+` and `=`; label it schematic or give a normalized example, `(|↑z⟩ + |↓z⟩)/√2`. On 52, replace “the control is 0 and 1 at once” with “the control is in `|+⟩` and the target is still in `|0⟩`.” On 13 and in the Bell notes, specify **this Bell state, both measured in the computational (Z) basis**: 00 and 11 each have probability ½. A classical 50:50 mixture gives the same counts; the coherent, nonfactorable state on slide 54 is the distinction. Its nonzero amplitudes are **exactly `1/√2`**, displayed as `0.7071`. The Q# example inspects them; it does not log measurement shots.
- **Slides 17–19 — distinguish the photographed hardware from the general idea.** On 17 use **QuTech**, not “QTech.” A cryostat photo does not establish a particular processor or a fixed-frequency design. Separate microwave drive, coupling and readout; do not imply error correction is already what makes every physical gate reliable. [QuTech's Starmon-5 fact sheet](https://qutech.nl/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Starmon5_FactSheet_240316.pdf) distinguishes drive lines, flux control and readout resonators. On 18 the cabinet is labeled **Pasqal ORION**: [Pasqal describes its Orion QPUs as analog](https://docs.pasqal.com/qpu-emulators/qpus/). Distinguish programmable Rydberg interactions there from digital entangling gates on some neutral-atom platforms. On 19, describe **a proposed Majorana-based encoding**: exchanging separated modes would transform the encoded state, not “exchange information.” A device photo or [fermion-parity measurement](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08445-2) is not evidence that the pictured device performed a protected braid. Remove or complete the dangling README sentence beginning “before the talk turns…”.
- **Opening, slides 22, 27–36 and 62 — say when the vector model applies.** A **pure** qubit is represented by a normalized vector in ℂ² up to global phase. The unit vectors modulo global phase form **CP¹**, not all of ℂ² modulo phase; CP¹ is a *projective space*, not another vector space. On the [Bloch sphere](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/learning/en/courses/general-formulation-of-quantum-information/density-matrices/bloch-sphere), `|+⟩` is halfway in *polar angle* on the equator, not at the centre of the ball (the maximally mixed state). Reword slide 32's “not halfway between the poles” description. Slide 29's README also promises the *same* projection everywhere: `+x` is horizontal there but slopes down-right on the gate diagrams. The axis meanings, not the camera angle, stay the same. For slide 36 distinguish **2ⁿ entries in an expanded column** from **2ⁿ independent inputs**: even a product state can have all entries nonzero while being specified by its one-qubit factors. On 62 say measurement in the chosen basis samples outcome *k* with probability `|aₖ|²`; it need not be uniform and can be certain. Match the closing SVG description to the takeaways actually shown.
- **Reader-facing prose and legibility.** Put the teaching point before the design commentary: for instance, README 7 can use the 41-vs-42 calculation instead of explaining the missing exclamation mark; 15 can explain what the refrigerator cools rather than the fades; 23 can explain amplitude versus probability rather than the colour gradient.
